Why this story stands out

The tension is sunk cost versus aliveness. The career still looks like something you are not supposed to walk away from, while Sunday nights keep arriving with the same dread.

What stands out is the refusal to treat quitting as the only form of seriousness. Conversations, a class, and a small side project are ways of gathering evidence before a household has to absorb a huge bet.

Coach-Halo perspective

Competence can hide a stalled yes

Being good at the work is part of why leaving feels disloyal. Promotions and a steady identity make the inner question look irrational. The body often answers first: dread, boredom in rooms that used to feel energizing, a wait for the feeling to pass.

A composite like this one is not a template. It is a reminder that career change can be treated as research. The point of a cheap experiment is not to produce a perfect story. It is to learn whether the imagined work survives contact with real hours.

The middle is allowed to be messy

Even a careful move can include less pay, more learning, and days of second-guessing. That does not mean the previous career was a waste. It means the next chapter is being built with incomplete information, which is normal.

If you are in a similar bind, the useful question is which uncertainty would shrink with one conversation, one class, or one bounded project, rather than with a midnight resignation.

Questions worth considering

  • What would you need to learn through a conversation, a class, or a small test before a bigger career decision felt clearer?
  • Which part of the current role is still worth protecting while you learn?

One possible next step

  • List the three questions a resignation would not answer, then pick one that a conversation or small project could answer.
  • Time-box a 90-day research period so the question does not float forever.
